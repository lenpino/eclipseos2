SWT Step 011. Trees

Objective

Implement the o.e.swt.widgets.Tree and TreeItem classes. The test case SWT011_01 checks the hierarchy, the indexes, the texts, the expansion, the bounds, the selection, the events, dispose() and removeAll(), the images and the colors automatically, and then shows a tree to be used by hand. SWT009_11 is the old visual test.

Task notes

The tree is the container control of PM (WC_CONTAINER, class #37) in its tree view, as the Workplace Shell uses it. The records of the container live in native memory and have pointers, so the previous approach, to copy them into Java objects (RECORDCORE) and send the copy, did not work: it released Java arrays with pointers that did not come from them and the VM aborted. Now:

  • A record is handled through its address (TreeItem.handle) with OS.malloc(), OS.free(), OS.memset() and OS.objcpy() with byte[], through Tree.peek() and poke(); CM_INSERTRECORD gets the address of the record that CM_ALLOCRECORD returned and of a RECORDINSERT built in memory.
  • The container keeps pointers to the texts of a record, so each text is allocated apart (the same one for the four views of the record) and freed when it is replaced and when the item is disposed.
  • The id of a TreeItem is stored in the extra bytes of its record (CM_ALLOCRECORD with 4), to find the item from a record when a notification arrives.
  • The events come as WM_CONTROL: CN_EMPHASIS is Selection, CN_ENTER is DefaultSelection, CN_EXPANDTREE and CN_COLLAPSETREE are Expand and Collapse. The container does not send them for what the program does, and a flag avoids it when the container does.
  • The selection (2026-11-13). The tree view of the container always has one selected record that cannot be deselected, and never more than one. With CA_OWNERDRAW the container sends WM_DRAWITEM for the icon (CMA_ICON) and the text (CMA_TEXT) of each record, with its emphasis in fsAttribute; when the owner draws the text and returns TRUE, the container draws no highlight (840 pixels of the highlight color in the row, then none but the 16 of the dotted frame of the cursor). So TreeItem has a selected flag, Tree draws the text with the highlight colors when it is set, and the record of the container is only the cursor. Control.WM_DRAWITEM gives the containers their WM_DRAWITEM.
  • A click (WM_BUTTON1DOWN of the tree, before the container) on the icon or the text of an item selects it, with Ctrl it is added or removed and with Shift the items from the anchor are selected (SWT.MULTI). The container does not notify a click on the record it already has selected, which after deselectAll() would be lost. The record of the row that was clicked, also on the plus sign, becomes the cursor: the CN_EMPHASIS that follows is not taken as a move of the keyboard, which is how a click on the plus sign no longer selects. A CN_EMPHASIS of another record is the keyboard: it selects that one.
  • The container does not expand a record when its plus sign gets the mouse messages from a program (WM_BUTTON1DOWN, UP and CLICK sent to it): it looks at the real mouse. The checks send the clicks on the items; the plus sign is tried by hand.
  • The colors of the items (2026-11-13) are drawn with the text: the foreground and the background of the item, or else those of the tree, in RGB values. The presentation space of the container is put in RGB mode (GpiCreateLogColorTable with LCOLF_RGB) for the text and given back its default table (LCOL_RESET); the images, the lines and the plus signs that the container draws after it are still right (checked on the screen). The rectangle of the text is 13 pixels high in a row of 16, so the background of an item is that band, as the label of Windows. A selected item has the colors of the highlight.
  • The check boxes (2026-11-13). The container has none, so the tree draws them with the text: a frame, gray inside when grayed, and a mark when checked. The container sizes the rectangle of the text by the text, so with SWT.CHECK the text of a record starts with twelve spaces (the native text only; getText() has none): the rectangle has room for the box and a gap, and the tree draws the text after the box. The width of a space of the font of the container is not known to the tree: a GC on the tree measured 3 pixels, the container uses about 2. A click on a box (in WM_BUTTON1DOWN, before the container gets it) and the space bar on the item with the cursor change it and send a Selection with SWT.CHECK; the selection does not change.

Three problems that were found on the way and are not tree specific are in lessons learned: 24 constants of OS.java written in decimal, "cDelta " in structs.c, and the SBS_VERT that Scrollable adds. The behavior that differs from Windows is in difference 013.
