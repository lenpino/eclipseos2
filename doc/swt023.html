SWT Step 023. The custom widgets

Objective

Build the custom widgets of Eclipse 2.0.1 (StyledText, CTabFolder, SashForm, CCombo, CLabel, TableTree, ViewForm and the others of org.eclipse.swt.custom), which are made of the other widgets in pure Java, and see them work on PM. The editors and the views of Eclipse are made of them. The test case SWT023_01 (26 automatic checks) types and clicks in a StyledText, copies and pastes, and reads the screen; its interactive part has editors in the tabs of a CTabFolder.

Task notes

  • The folder Eclipse SWT Custom Widgets/common of the original distribution is compiled in both builds, and classes.inc lists its 47 classes.
  • What they need that the port did not have: RTFTransfer (the registered format "Rich Text Format", the name of Windows, see step 022), the emulated BidiUtil of Eclipse SWT/emulated/bidi (no bidirectional text, as on Motif and GTK), PrinterData of the original distribution, and a Printer and a PrintDialog without printers, those of GTK in 2.0.1: printing is not ported, StyledText.print() fails as it does on GTK.
  • The first new StyledText() failed with a division by zero in ScrollBar.getThumb(): a canvas had no scroll bars. They are step 024.
  • A check of the test was wrong, not the port: a backspace that a program posts must be as PM sends it, a virtual key and a character (8); with the virtual key only, StyledText got no character.
  • The second test case, SWT023_02 (25 checks): ScrolledComposite (the bars by the size of the content, setOrigin(), scrolling with the bars, hidden bars when the content fits, the expanded content), ViewForm and CLabel (layout, size, the text on the screen on the left and on the right), TableTree, TableEditor, StackLayout and BusyIndicator.
  • Found by TableTree: a table sent no mouse events for the clicks on its rows. They go to the window of the records of the container (CID_LEFTDVWND), which is not a window of SWT; TableTree expands on a MouseDown on the plus sign. Display.filterMessage() gives the buttons of that window to the table (Table.recordsMouse()), with the point in the table, as Control does for its own window; the container still gets them. Checked with a click posted to that window.
