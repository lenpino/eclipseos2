SWT Step 020. CoolBar

Objective

Have CoolBar and CoolItem. PM has no rebar control: the port takes the emulated cool bar of Eclipse 2.0.1 (the one of Motif, in pure Java, folder Eclipse SWT/emulated/coolbar), which draws the grabbers and moves the items itself. The test case SWT020_01 has 47 automatic checks (the layout of the items, the pixels of the grabbers on the screen, dragging, double click, locked, setItemLayout, the chevron and disposing) and an interactive part: a cool bar with three tool bars and a combo, the chevrons showing a menu with the hidden buttons, and a check box to lock it.

Task notes

  • The emulated classes compile as they are: they go into the build from their own source folder (classes.inc, both build.xml). What they need from the port is plain SWT: mouse events (step 016), cursors (step 015), redraw of a part, the clipping of the paint, tool bars and images.
  • The dragging of the automatic part sends the mouse events to the bar (notifyListeners); the pointer is not moved. The pixels are read from the screen, so the mouse must not be over the window meanwhile. An item dragged under its row makes a new row and the bar grows: the controls of the first row stay at the top (in PM the children keep their distance to the bottom of the parent, but the bar lays the items out again after its new size).
  • Three defects of the port found by the test, fixed:
    • Control.setVisible(false) did nothing while the shell was not open yet: it returned early when WinIsWindowVisible was already false, and that is false while any parent is hidden. The chevrons hidden while the bar was built showed up when the shell was opened. Now it looks at the WS_VISIBLE bit of the control, as Windows does.
    • getBorderWidth() gave the border of the system (1) to every control; on Windows it is 0 without SWT.BORDER. The composites of PM draw no border, and now give 0 without SWT.BORDER: the bar was 2 pixels higher than its row.
    • COLOR_WIDGET_NORMAL_SHADOW was the color of the scroll bars (192, nearly the background, 204) and COLOR_WIDGET_LIGHT_SHADOW that of the selected menu item. They are now the dark and middle colors of the buttons of PM (SYSCLR_BUTTONDARK, SYSCLR_BUTTONMIDDLE), the 3D colors of Windows.
  • Three more, seen on the screen in the interactive part (the automatic checks did not look at them yet; now they do):
    • The composites had no WS_CLIPCHILDREN (it was commented out in Composite.widgetStyle(); Windows has it): a redraw of the bar, after a drag or setLocked, painted over its tool bars and their text was gone. The test counts the dark pixels of a tool bar before and after redraw() (182 and 0 before the fix).
    • getSize() of a drop down Combo gave the window of PM, which includes the list (122 pixels for a field of 20), and the row of the bar was that high. On Windows it is the height of the text field; now it is too.
    • A consequence of the clipping, seen on the screen the next day (green under the combo of SWT009_04 and of the cool bar): nothing painted the part of the window of a drop down combo below its text field, which the parent painted before, and the screen behind showed through. The combo paints it now with the background of its parent while the list is closed. The test opens a window with a combo over a black one and reads the pixels below the field. As before, that part hides the controls under it.
    • A ToolItem was at least 24 x 22 also with an image. The bar measures the trim of its chevrons with a tool bar with an image of one pixel (on Windows about 7), so it got 21 and the chevron of the combo item failed with an image of a negative height. The minimum is now only for the items without an image, as on Windows.
  • A mistake of the first version of the test: setControl puts the control into the item, still empty, so its size has to be taken before (as the examples of Eclipse do).

The differences with Windows are in difference 022.
